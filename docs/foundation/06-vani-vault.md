> Foundation handbook v1.0 · October 2026 · Source transcription, not a revision.
> Paragraph text is preserved; Word layout and typography are not reproduced.

Purpose

The Vani Vault is not an editorial calendar. It is a place where unfinished spiritual attention can remain unfinished.

The goal is to capture questions before they harden into opinions.

Vault sections

1. Questions

Things you genuinely do not know.

2. Seeds

A sentence, contradiction, image, memory, or line that has energy but not yet an argument.

3. Texts

Verses and passages that keep calling you back.

4. Stories

Episodes from the Mahābhārata, Rāmāyaṇa, Purāṇas, lives of saints, or oral traditions that deserve later study.

5. Terms

Words whose translations are deceptively simple: dharma, karma, māyā, vairāgya, śraddhā, smaraṇa, bhakti, jñāna, mokṣa, tapas, prasāda, līlā.

6. Frictions

Places where spiritual ideals and actual life disagree.

Examples: wanting praise while practicing “non-attachment”; getting angry while writing about compassion; using surrender to avoid a difficult conversation.

7. Experiences

Moments from work, family, travel, ritual, grief, parenting, conflict, beauty, fatigue, prayer, or silence that might later reveal a larger question.

8. Source Cards

Verified source notes. Keep research separate from memory.

9. Dormant Series

Good names and concepts that are not yet mature enough to become public commitments.

10. Drafts

Pieces that have moved beyond a seed but are not ready for publication.

Seed Card template

Seed:

One sentence only.

What happened / what triggered it?

Concrete context.

The question underneath it:

What do I actually not know?

What might this connect to?

Text, term, story, school, practice, ordinary-life tension.

What am I tempted to conclude too quickly?

A built-in bias check.

What would I need to verify?

Scripture, Sanskrit, attribution, history, commentary, scientific/psychological claim.

Leave it with:

One unresolved sentence.

First 24 questions for the Vault

These are deliberately phrased as questions rather than doctrines.

1. Who am I when the roles I use to introduce myself become quiet?

2. If I can notice a thought, what is the relationship between the thought and the one noticing it?

3. What does dharma mean when every available choice seems to cost someone something?

4. Can attachment disguise itself as love?

5. Can detachment disguise itself as avoidance?

6. Why does Krishna ask Arjuna to act rather than escape the battlefield?

7. What do I really mean when I say I want “peace”?

8. Can ambition and sādhanā live in the same person without hypocrisy?

9. What is māyā in an age where identity can be curated all day long?

10. When does self-improvement become another project of the ego?

11. Is wanting to become “more spiritual” still a form of becoming?

12. What does surrender ask of me after I have done everything I reasonably can?

13. What is the difference between acceptance and passivity?

14. What does remembrance of the Divine look like on an ordinary, uneventful day?

15. Why is bhakti vulnerable?

16. What do I do with prayer when nothing changes?

17. What changes when I compare Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja, and Madhva rather than assuming “Vedānta” means one thing?

18. What does mokṣa mean before I turn it into a modern wellness metaphor?

19. Why do sacred stories survive when philosophical summaries are easier to explain?

20. When I say a deity “represents” something, where did that interpretation actually come from?

21. What does karma yoga look like when somebody else receives the recognition?

22. What does ahiṃsā require in my internal speech?

23. What does aparigraha mean in a world built to remove friction from buying?

24. Would I still pursue this spiritual practice if nobody ever knew I practiced it?

Dormant series vault

Keep these names without committing to them:

Gita in the Grocery Store — the Gītā under ordinary pressure.

Bhakti in the Broken — devotion in loss, helplessness, and longing.

Maya in the Feed — attention, identity, comparison, algorithms, and desire.

Neti Neti Notebook — experiments in examining identity.

Seeker's Confession — where practice and self-image collide.

Letters to the Inner Sage — dialogic reflective pieces.

Mothers of the Mahabharata — women of the epic approached as complex figures, not flattened symbols.

Manidweepam Meditations — only after careful textual research into the relevant Śākta sources and commentaries.

A series leaves the Vault only after the writing has already made it real.
