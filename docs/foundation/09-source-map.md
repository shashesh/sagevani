# 09 — Initial Source Map

> Foundation handbook v1.0 · October 2026 · Source transcription, not a revision.
> Paragraph text is preserved; Word layout and typography are not reproduced.

This is a working research map, not a canon list. Add sources as SageVani develops expertise in particular texts and traditions.

A. Text and commentary tools

Gita Supersite — IIT Kanpur

Use for: Bhagavad Gītā Sanskrit text, translations, and multiple classical/contemporary commentaries in one place. It includes commentarial material associated with Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja, Madhva, Abhinavagupta, Vallabha, Madhusūdana Sarasvatī, Śrīdhara Svāmī and others.

Why it matters for SageVani: it makes disagreement visible. A verse need not be silently read through one school.

URL: https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/

Caution: the presence of multiple commentaries does not remove the need to identify which commentary you are using.

GRETIL — Göttingen Register of Electronic Texts in Indian Languages

Use for: searchable electronic Sanskrit texts, including the Bhāgavata Purāṇa and many other works.

URL: https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/

Caution: text-critical status varies by item. Treat it as a research tool, not automatically as the definitive critical edition.

Cologne Digital Sanskrit Dictionaries — University of Cologne

Use for: lexical research across digitized Sanskrit dictionaries, including Monier-Williams and others.

URL: https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/

Caution: dictionary glosses are starting points. Philosophical meaning depends on textual and commentarial context.

B. Philosophy and historical context

Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Hindu Philosophy

Use for: orientation to schools, philosophers, concepts, and differences among Vedāntic systems.

URL: https://iep.utm.edu/hindu-ph/

Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Rāmānuja

Use for: a scholarly orientation to Viśiṣṭādvaita and Rāmānuja's philosophical project.

URL: https://iep.utm.edu/ramanuja/

Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Madhva

Use for: a scholarly orientation to Dvaita and Madhva's critique of identity between ātman and Brahman.

URL: https://iep.utm.edu/madhva/

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Śaṅkara

Use for: a scholarly overview of Śaṅkara, Advaita, liberation, superimposition, and later debates.

URL: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/shankara/

Caution for all encyclopedias: these are secondary sources. For close claims about a classical thinker, follow their references into primary/commentarial scholarship when necessary.

C. Tradition-specific sources

Tradition-specific websites and publications are valuable when SageVani is trying to understand how a living community reads a text.

Examples may include:

Advaita Ashrama / Ramakrishna Order publications;

Śrī Vaiṣṇava publications and teachers;

Madhva/Dvaita institutions;

Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava sources;

Śaiva and Śākta lineages;

recognized temple and monastic publications.

Rule: name the tradition. A tradition-specific explanation should not be described as “the Hindu view.”

Vedabase

Use for: Bhāgavata and Bhagavad Gītā material in the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava/ISKCON tradition, especially A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda's translations and purports.

URL: https://vedabase.io/

Caution: this is a valuable lineage source, not a neutral cross-tradition reference. Label it accordingly.

D. Printed editions and books

As SageVani matures, build a personal shelf rather than relying entirely on websites.

For every major scripture you use repeatedly, aim eventually to own or consistently consult:

1. a readable translation;

2. at least one traditional commentary relevant to the school being studied;

3. a scholarly translation or study where useful;

4. the Sanskrit text or a reliable digital equivalent.

Keep edition details in Source Cards so quotations can be traced later.

E. Research rules by claim type

“What does this verse say?”

Primary text + translation comparison.

“What does this Sanskrit word mean?”

Lexicon + passage context + commentary when philosophically loaded.

“What does Hinduism/Sanātana Dharma believe?”

Usually rewrite the question. Identify the specific schools/texts/traditions involved.

“What does this symbol mean?”

Find textual/traditional evidence. If absent, label the reading contemplative or modern.

“How old is this practice/phrase?”

Use historical scholarship, not devotional repetition.

“What did this saint/teacher say?”

Find a primary publication, authenticated transcript, or reliable collected works before using quote graphics or quotation sites.

F. Research log fields

For every source used in a substantial piece, record:

author / commentator;

work title;

editor / translator;

tradition or school when relevant;

publication or hosting institution;

passage / page / verse;

URL and access date for web sources;

what exact claim the source supports;

whether the source is primary, traditional commentary, academic, living-tradition, or general secondary.

Good citation practice begins while researching, not after the draft is finished.
